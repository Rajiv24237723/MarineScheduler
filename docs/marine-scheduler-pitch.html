The problem

The month always changes. The plan can't keep up.

Coastal marine planning still runs on spreadsheets, noon-reports and phone calls. The start-of-month plan looks balanced — then a berth closes, a tanker slips, demand spikes, and the schedule has to be rebuilt by hand, under pressure, with no read on cost or risk.

Capability 02 — optimisation

Plan the whole month — re-plan any horizon

Voyage plan · frozen vs re-solved

A greedy-plus-GRASP engine builds a full month of voyages — ballast out, multi-pickup, multi-drop, back empty — the cheapest feasible hull for each lift, in seconds.

When something breaks mid-month, it freezes every committed leg and re-solves only the remaining horizon. The month continues as an immutable child version — it never restarts from scratch.

Capability 03 — real constraints

It respects the infrastructure, not just the map

Berths · draft × tide · closures

Berth simultaneity, pump rate and load-dependent draft against the tide are hard constraints in the solve. A hull is never scheduled into a berth that can't take its draft, or beyond a terminal's parallel-berth limit.

Outages and date-range closures — an SPM shut for swell, a berth down for maintenance — flow straight in. Nothing is routed into a closed berth; demurrage is charged when a hull must wait one out.
